# S8.5 — THE MVP ROUND LOOP: design audit

No code, no migration, no UI change. What ran: the existing suite, the live
schema and database, and simulations driving the **real** `simulateBattle`,
`pairRound` and `computeSynergy` against the real catalogue.

This document supersedes parts of `docs/S8.5-DESIGN.md`. That one asked "how do
the existing matchups fight?". This one asks "what makes the round loop a game?"
— and three of the answers changed.

---

## 0. HEADLINE

Three findings decide everything below, and two of them are gaps I shipped.

**A. Rounds 6–8 buy nothing.** `dw_record_acquisition` puts a player's first
five characters on the board and everything after on the bench, and **no
migration ever updates or deletes a `board_slots` row.** `fightingBoardOf`
excludes the bench. So the sixth, seventh and eighth purchases *can never
fight*. The late game the brief describes — "read your opponents and push the
board to its ceiling" — is currently impossible, and S8.2's "R6–R8 acquisition
is a real decision" is a decision between wasting credits and saving them.

**B. The synergy system already exists, and it already produces the
early/mid/late arc.** `computeSynergy` scores tag groups per category,
`perMember × (n−1)`, capped at +10%. Measured: at one character 0% of teams have
any synergy, at three 59%, at five 96%; a deliberately stacked board reaches the
cap at four characters. Nothing needs inventing — §7 asks only whether the cap
is the right size.

**C. A decided match does not end.** `dw_match_next_phase` forks to
`CHAMPIONSHIP` only when `round >= round_count`. With one survivor left at round
six, the match walks rounds seven and eight pairing nobody and doing nothing.

And the calibration the brief asked for: **80 HP with the measured damage curve**
lands a five-player table on ~3 survivors at round 8, with eliminations spread
evenly across the last three rounds rather than massed in one.

## 1. CURRENT ARCHITECTURE

### A) What state exists for match HP and lives

```sql
match_players (
  match_id, player_id,
  hp            int not null default 100,   -- the life total
  credits       int not null,               -- match wallet, separate
  round_wins    int not null default 0,
  streak        int not null default 0,
  eliminated_at int,                        -- round number, null while alive
  modifiers     jsonb
)
```

`hp` and `eliminated_at` exist and are read by `pairRound` (a seat with
`eliminated_at` or `hp <= 0` is excluded) and by every `v_live` guard in SQL.
**Nothing writes them yet.** They have sat at 100 in every live match.

`clampHp(hp, roundNo)` and `ELIMINATION_FROM_ROUND = 6` already exist in
`rounds.ts`, tested, unused.

### B) How the tables support the loop

| Table | Supports | Verified live |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `match_players` | HP, elimination, match wallet, streak | 3–5 rows per match, credits move, hp does not |
| `match_acquisitions` | every purchase: round, player, character, price, seed | 29 rows across 8 rounds in a real 4-player match |
| `board_slots` | who owns what, zone, slot | accumulates correctly; **never updated after insert** |
| `round_matchups` | pairing, ratings, reason, and an empty `battle_result` | 16 rows in an 8-round 5-player match |
| `games` (per round) | the round's draft, `characters_per_player = 1` | one per round |

The accumulation the brief wants (1 → 2 → 3 → …) is real and verified: board
rows persist across rounds and the auction quota is one per round.

### C) How `simulateBattle` computes a team fight

```
per character:  projectAxes (crossover normalisation)
             →  applyFormation            ±4% on two axes
             →  × environmentMultiplier   map tags + event card
             →  − event stat drain
             →  maxHp = 70 + defense×1.55 + power×0.45

per team:       synergy = computeSynergy(chars).total, capped at +10%
                attack  = (power×0.6 + special×0.2 + strategy×0.2) × (1 + synergy)

round loop:     max 14 rounds, speed-ordered initiative, 40% focus-fire on the
                weakest enemy, special / crit / block rolls, fights to
                elimination
```

Measured over 4,000 real 5v5s: the winner keeps a mean of **3.07** fighters and
the loser is **always wiped to 0%**. The engine already reports
`teams[].remainingHpPct`, `survivors`, `winProbability`, `upset`, `mvp`,
`turningPoint` and per-character results.

### I) Data loss between auction and battle board

**Yes — and this is finding A.** The chain is

```
auction sale → match_acquisitions (all of them)
             → board_slots  zone = FRONT for the first five, BENCH after
             → fightingBoardOf  drops BENCH, caps at 5
```

Everything a player buys is *recorded*; only the first five can ever *fight*.
Nothing promotes a bench character. A round-7 purchase stronger than every
character on the board sits behind them for the rest of the match.

## 2. WHAT IS MISSING

| # | Gap | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Nothing writes `match_players.hp` | no life system at all; every match ends 100/100/100 |
| 2 | Nothing writes `round_matchups.battle_result` | no fights, no winners, no results screen content |
| 3 | Bench characters can never be promoted | rounds 6–8 buy nothing; no late game |
| 4 | No early-end when one player is left | a decided match walks empty rounds |
| 5 | `COMBAT` has no completion guard | a round can be walked past unfought (same shape as the auction hole 0033 just closed) |
| 6 | `FINAL_COMBAT` has no bracket | the last round has no shape |
| 7 | No HP or elimination anywhere in the UI | the player rail shows a number that never moves |

Items 1, 2 and 5 are S8.5b/c. Item 3 is the one this brief adds and it is not
optional: without it the loop the brief describes does not exist.

## 3. THE PROPOSED LIFE SYSTEM

**Starting HP: 80.** Measured, not chosen. Sweep over the real engine, 200
matches per cell, elimination allowed from round 6:

| HP | curve | 5P survivors at R8 | eliminations/match | matches ending early |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 60 | C | 2.19 | 2.81 | 12% |
| **80** | **C** | **3.08** | **1.85** | **0%** |
| 100 | C | 3.93 | 1.07 | 0% |
| 80 | harsh | 2.11 | 2.89 | 14% |

At 60 the table is thinned too fast and one match in eight is over before round
8. At 100 barely anybody dies — a five-player match loses one player all game
and the championship is four-handed. **80 leaves three players standing for the
final, which is the bracket the rulebook already describes.**

| Table | survivors at R8 | eliminations | matches ending before R8 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3P | 1.91 | 1.10 | 22% |
| 4P | 2.37 | 1.65 | 5% |
| 5P | 3.08 | 1.85 | 0% |

**HP is completely separate from credits.** `match_players.hp` and
`match_players.credits` are different columns, written by different rules;
`players.credits` remains the untouched legacy wallet. Nothing converts between
life and money in either direction, and a test asserts combat never mentions
credits.

## 4. THE ROUND DAMAGE FORMULA

### D) What decreases when a player loses a round

**Only `match_players.hp`.** Not credits, not board, not acquisitions. The
loser keeps everything they bought — losing a round costs you life, not your
team, which is what makes the next auction still worth playing.

```
damage = clamp( round( (6 + 14 × winnerRemainingHpFraction)
                       × (1 + 0.12 × (round − 1))
                       × (upset ? 1.15 : 1) ), 4, 24 )

hp = clampHp(hp − damage, round)     -- floors at 1 before round 6, 0 after
```

`winnerRemainingHpFraction` is `teams[winner].remainingHpPct / 100` — the
engine's own number, copied. A winner who barely survived costs their opponent
less than one who walked through them. This is the "remaining enemy power" the
brief asks for, and it is a far better-distributed signal than a survivor count
that clusters at three.

Measured with 80 HP: mean damage 15.3–15.9, median 15–16, worst case 24.

### L) Why this curve and not a harsher one

Harsher damage produces *more* comeback but a worse arc:

| curve | 4P eliminations by round | comeback | matches ending early |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| **C (chosen)** | **R6:142 R7:88 R8:97** | 8% | 5% |
| mid | R6:211 R7:101 R8:103 | 14% | 22% |
| harsh | R6:276 R7:97 R8:102 | 17% | 43% |

The harsher curves mass their eliminations in round 6 — the round the floor
lifts — and then 22–43% of matches are already over. Curve C spreads them
evenly across the last three rounds, which is the tension the brief is asking
for.

**The honest finding: the damage curve cannot fix comeback.** At five players it
is 3% under every curve tested, and softening the curve makes it *worse* (1% at
100 HP) because a lead compounds over more rounds with nothing resetting it.
This is the fourth milestone to reach the same conclusion — matchmaking did not
move it either. Comeback belongs to events, bounties and the underdog reward
(S8.8), and this document does not pretend otherwise.

## 5. ELIMINATION RULES

### E) When elimination happens

At the end of a round's **resolution**, after every matchup of that round has
been applied — never mid-round. Two matchups resolving in the same round cannot
each eliminate the same player, because the pairing gives every live seat
exactly one matchup.

* `hp` reaches 0 **and** `round >= 6` ⇒ `eliminated_at = round`.
* Before round 6, `clampHp` floors at 1. Nobody leaves the table in the first
  five rounds.
* `eliminated_at` is written **only when it is currently null**, inside the same
  locked transaction. A second write cannot re-eliminate.

**Measured: the floor is nearly free.** Lowering it from round 6 to round 4
changes almost nothing — with curve C at 80 HP nobody's HP reaches zero before
round 5 anyway (4P: 42 eliminations move from R6 to R5; survivors 2.37 → 2.35;
comeback 8% → 7%). The locked rule costs the design nothing and keeps a friend
from watching four rounds from the sidelines.

### An eliminated player

* is excluded from `pairRound` (already true and tested);
* takes no part in the next auction — their round demand must be excluded from
  `dw_round_demand` (already written that way: it filters `eliminated_at is null`);
* their board is never used as an opponent, because they are never paired;
* cannot be revived — `eliminated_at` is write-once.

### M) When one player is left

**A gap.** `dw_match_next_phase` forks to `CHAMPIONSHIP` only at
`round >= round_count`, so a match decided at round six walks two empty rounds.

Proposed: the round-end transition also forks to `MATCH_RESULTS` when fewer than
two seats remain, and the survivor is the champion. This is a change to the
phase machine's single fork — the one place the round number already decides a
branch — and needs a mirrored change in `rounds.ts` plus its transition tests.

## 6. THREE, FOUR AND FIVE PLAYERS

### F) Three players: matchup and encounter

One duel and one encounter, every round, with the odd seat rotating — measured
live: byes spread to within one across eight rounds, zero consecutive rematches.

The encounter is a real `simulateBattle` against a board the server builds at
the field's median board power, with full damage on a loss. Calibrated: an
encounter round costs **−1.2 HP (3P) / +0.2 HP (5P)** relative to a duel round,
which is neutral. The knob is steep — at 0.92× the seat wins 87% of its rounds,
at 1.08× it wins 6% — so the construction is tested against the engine rather
than trusted.

| Table | Shape | Encounters per round | Survivors at R8 |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 3P | 1 duel + 1 encounter | 1 | 1.91 |
| 4P | 2 duels | 0 (until an elimination makes it odd) | 2.37 |
| 5P | 2 duels + 1 encounter | 1 | 3.08 |

### N) Two players

Currently supported: six rounds, one pairing every round, no encounter. It
works and the tests cover it.

**Recommendation: keep it working, exclude it from the MVP framing.** With two
players there is no elimination arc, no bracket and no "remaining opponents" —
it is a six-round duel, which is a fine thing but not the game this brief
describes. The lobby should present the match mode from three players up and let
two players keep the classic single-battle game. No code needs removing; this is
a labelling decision.

## 7. SYNERGY — MVP DESIGN

### J) It already exists. Here it is.

```ts
computeSynergy(chars):
  for each tag carried by 2+ characters:
    bonus += perMember × (count − 1)
  total = min(0.10, bonus)          // MAX_SYNERGY
```

Every category defines 5–6 tag groups at `perMember` 0.02–0.025 — Avengers,
X-Men, villains, cosmic; pack, predator, herd; playmaker, striker, technical;
shooter, rim, lockdown. The bonus multiplies attack inside the round loop and
`teamRating` outside it, and the engine already names the groups on the result
(`teams[].synergies`, e.g. `"Villains ×3"`).

**Measured against the real catalogue:**

| Board size | teams with any synergy | mean bonus | deliberately stacked |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0% | 0.000 | +0.0% |
| 2 | 26% | 0.007 | +2.5% |
| 3 | 59% | 0.018 | +5.0% |
| 4 | 84% | 0.035 | **+10.0% (cap)** |
| 5 | 96% | 0.055 | +10.0% |

This is exactly the shape the brief asks for and nobody has to build it: round
one is pure character quality, synergy becomes reachable at two, meaningful at
three, and a player who drafts for it hits the ceiling at four.

**MVP decision: change nothing about synergy.** No new bonuses, no invented
combos, no per-character specials. The one open question is whether +10% is
large enough for "cheap combo" to genuinely beat "one expensive star" — and
that is measurable once combat runs, not before. The extension seam already
exists: adding a group is one object in a category's `synergies` array, and
adding a *kind* of synergy is a new entry in `SYNERGY_BY_TAG`.

## 8. EARLY / MID / LATE GAME

### K) Where the phases come from

They are not new mechanics. They fall out of three rules that already exist plus
the one fix in §2.

| | Rounds | What the board is | What the decision is | Driven by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Early** | 1–2 | 1–2 characters, no synergy possible | "is this hero good on its own?" | acquisition mandatory; synergy = 0 at one character |
| **Mid** | 3–5 | 3–5, synergy reachable and then capped | "does my combination work?" | 59% → 96% of boards carry synergy; board fills at five |
| **Late** | 6–8 | 5 fighting, up to 3 in reserve | "can I upgrade what fights?" | acquisition optional, elimination live, **requires the bench fix** |

**Early** already works: a single character has no synergy and no formation
depth, so round one is a pure read on quality, and the reserve rule makes
overpaying for it a real cost.

**Mid** already works: the board fills, tags start to line up, and the cap
arrives at four for anybody drafting deliberately.

**Late does not work today** and is finding A. Without a way to promote a bench
character, a round-7 purchase is dead credits and the only late decision is
whether to save money. The minimum fix that creates a late game:

> **Auto-promote on strength, until S8.4 gives the player the choice.** When a
> purchase would land on the bench, compare it with the weakest character on the
> board by `gamePower`; if it is stronger, swap them. Deterministic, needs no UI,
> and turns rounds 6–8 into "is this worth displacing what I have?".

That is a stopgap with an honest cost: it takes the decision away from the
player, which is the opposite of what S8.4 is for. The alternative is to build
board management now. **Recommendation: auto-promote in S8.5b, and let S8.4
replace it with a real choice** — a dead late game is worse than a temporarily
automatic one.

## 9. DATABASE CHANGES

Nothing new to store. Every column the loop needs already exists.

| Change | Where | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dw_resolve_matchup(room, pairing_index, result)` | new function | writes `battle_result`, `winner_player_id`, `damage`, `started_at`, `settled_at`, applies HP and elimination — all in one transaction |
| `dw_advance_match_phase` | replace | `COMBAT_INCOMPLETE` guard, and the one-survivor fork |
| `dw_match_tick` | replace | `NEEDS_COMBAT` report |
| `dw_match_snapshot` | replace | expose `battleResult` and `startedAt` per matchup |
| `dw_record_acquisition` | replace | the bench promotion of §8 |
| `dw_match_next_phase` | replace | the one-survivor fork |

**Deliberately not added:** a lives column separate from `hp` (it *is* the
lives), a per-matchup status (`battle_result IS NULL` is the status), a copy of
either board (`board_slots` holds it and `battle_result.combatants` names every
fighter), a damage log (the result carries it).

The guard 0033 just closed is the template: `if not found` matters as much as
`if status = 'ACTIVE'`, and the pairing guard already had it right.

## 10. CONCURRENCY MODEL

### G) Atomic HP updates with several fights in one round

**One lock: the match row.** Every resolution takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on
`matches`, so the two or three matchups of a round serialise. Three short
transactions per round is a price worth paying to remove every question about
two fights racing to change the same player's HP or to eliminate the same seat.

```
dw_resolve_matchup(room, pairing_index, result):
  lock matches FOR UPDATE
  phase must be COMBAT or FINAL_COMBAT
  read the row for (match, current round, pairing_index)
  if battle_result is not null  -> return { noop, alreadyResolved }   -- one matchup, one resolution
  write result, winner, damage, started_at, settled_at
  apply hp to the loser with clampHp
  round_wins / streak
  eliminated_at, only if currently null
  bump state_version
```

**Invariants the tests must prove, not assume:**

* two matchups of one round can never write the same player's HP — the pairing
  gives every live seat exactly one matchup, and a test resolves one matchup and
  asserts every other seat's HP is byte-equal;
* 2, 6 and 10 concurrent resolutions of one matchup produce exactly one
  authoritative result and no second damage, elimination or reward;
* a losing resolver is told it lost rather than reported as a success — the
  mistake S8.2 shipped and S8.3 fixed;
* `COMBAT → ROUND_END` is refused while any matchup is unresolved, on both the
  clock path and the host path, proved by deleting a result behind the server's
  back.

## 12. GO / NO-GO CRITERIA

Beyond the fourteen already recorded in `docs/S8.5-DESIGN.md` §25, the MVP loop
adds:

1. A round produces, for every player, all of: current HP, eliminated flag,
   board, acquisition history, current matchup, battle result, damage taken and
   remaining opponents — and the next round consumes them deterministically.
2. Two matchups in one round provably never write the same player's HP.
3. An eliminated player: takes no part in the next auction, is never paired, is
   never used as an opponent, and cannot be revived. Each asserted separately.
4. HP never negative, never below 1 before round 6, `eliminated_at` write-once.
5. A match with one survivor ends at that round, with that player as champion.
6. Rounds 6–8 purchases can reach the fighting board — the §8 fix, verified by a
   test that buys a stronger character late and sees it fight.
7. Board accumulation 1 → 2 → 3 → … verified live at 3P, 4P and 5P.
8. `players.credits` untouched; `match_players.credits` untouched by combat; HP
   never derived from either.
9. Measured on the live build: 5P ends with 2–4 survivors; eliminations spread
   across the last three rounds rather than massed in one.
10. Deterministic seed, mutation set caught, `liveDefinitionOf` used for every
    SQL assertion, production build clean, mobile 375/390 px.

**No GO is claimed here.** This is an audit; three of its findings are gaps in
code already deployed, and one of them (the bench) changes what S8.5b has to
build.
