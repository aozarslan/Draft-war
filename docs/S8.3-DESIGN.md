# S8.3 — MATCHMAKING: design and audit

No production code was written for this document. What ran: the existing test
suite, the live schema, and two simulations in a scratchpad driving the real
catalogue's `gamePower` distribution — 10,000 matches per configuration.

**The headline finding is not about pairing.** All three candidate algorithms
pair players acceptably. What separates them is two things a naive reading
misses: how often the *same two people* meet twice in a row, and how unevenly
the odd seat's free round is distributed. On a three-player table, the two
simpler algorithms hand one player up to **seven byes out of eight rounds** —
they play one real match all game. The chosen algorithm caps that at two.

**The second finding is that a bye is worth about twenty points of championship
probability**, and no amount of pairing cleverness fixes that. It is a combat
rule, not a matchmaking rule, and §7 spends its evidence there.

---

## 1. MATCHMAKING RULES

### 1.1 By table size

| Players | Shape | Odd seat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | A vs B, every round | — |
| 3 | one duel + one odd seat | rotates |
| 4 | two duels | — |
| 5 | two duels + one odd seat | rotates |

**Two players is not a matchmaking problem.** There is exactly one pairing, so
every round is a rematch and every rematch is consecutive. The simulation
confirms the obvious: rematch 83.3%, consecutive 83.3%, identical across all
three algorithms. The design consequence is that **the rematch penalty must not
fire at two players** — a rule that can never be satisfied is a rule that
produces a permanent, meaningless warning. The pairing function short-circuits
before scoring when `live.length === 2`.

**Three players: one duel and one odd seat, not a three-way.** A three-way
battle is available in the engine — `simulateBattle` takes N teams — but it is
rejected for two reasons. First, the arena is two-sided by construction:
`sceneAt` maps every seat to side 0 or side 1, and the S8 audit measured five of
fifteen sprites landing on identical coordinates in a three-team fight. Second,
a three-way makes "who beat whom" ambiguous exactly when HP damage needs an
author. One duel plus one odd seat keeps every fight 1v1, which is the shape the
renderer, the damage model and the replay all already understand.

### 1.2 Self-matching

Structurally impossible rather than checked. The pairing function consumes a
list and removes both seats when it forms a pair, so a seat cannot appear on
both sides of one matchup. The database backs it with
`check (player_b is null or player_b <> player_a)`, and a test asserts both.

### 1.3 Repeat opponents

Three separate pressures, in descending weight:

1. **Consecutive rematch** — meeting the same person two rounds running. The
   heaviest penalty. Measured at **0.0–0.8%** under the chosen algorithm against
   8–29% under the alternatives.
2. **Total rematch** — meeting them at all before everyone else has been met.
   Penalised proportionally to how many times it has already happened.
3. **Bye repetition** — taking the odd seat more often than anyone else.

On a small table these cannot all be satisfied. Three players over eight rounds
have only three distinct pairings; some rematch is arithmetic, not a failure.
The design target is therefore **"never twice in a row, and spread evenly
otherwise"**, not "never twice".

### 1.4 Variety as the match goes on

Variety is a consequence of the cost function rather than a separate mechanism:
each meeting raises the cost of meeting again, so the cheapest matching is
always the one that explores an unused pairing first. At five players the
measured total rematch rate falls from 46.6% (random) to **37.0%**.

## 2. EFFECTIVE TEAM RATING

### 2.1 What it is for

The rating exists for **one purpose**: to be the `|rating(a) − rating(b)|` term
in the pairing cost. It is not a score, not a ladder, and not shown as a verdict.
It never reaches combat — `simulateBattle` computes its own `teamRating` from
axes, and nothing in this document touches that.

### 2.2 The formula

```
rating(p) = Wpower · boardPower(p)
          + Wcount · rosterFill(p)
          + Wperf  · recentForm(p)
          + Whp    · healthShare(p)

boardPower(p)  = sum of the top-5 gamePower on the board / 500     → 0..1
rosterFill(p)  = min(1, boardSize / 5)                             → 0..1
recentForm(p)  = exponential average of round outcomes, seeded 0.5  → 0..1
healthShare(p) = hp / 100                                          → 0..1
```

### 2.3 Weights, and why

| Term | Weight | Reasoning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `boardPower` | **0.55** | The thing the player actually built, and the only term that survives into the fight. Dominant, but under half, so a strong board is not the whole answer. |
| `rosterFill` | **0.20** | Board *size* matters independently of quality in rounds 1–5, when a player with four fighters against five is short a body regardless of their gamePower. Fades to a constant from round 5 onward, which is correct: once everyone is full it stops discriminating. |
| `recentForm` | **0.15** | Two players with identical boards are not identically dangerous if one has won four rounds. Deliberately the smallest behavioural term so a hot streak nudges the pairing rather than deciding it. |
| `healthShare` | **0.10** | Lowest on purpose. HP is not strength — it is *remaining life*. Weighting it heavily would pair the wounded with the wounded and build a losers' bracket nobody asked for. At 0.10 it separates a 100 HP player from a 12 HP player only when their boards are otherwise close. |

### 2.4 What is inert in S8.3, and honestly so

**`recentForm` has no data source until S8.5** and **positioning has none until
S8.7.** Round combat does not exist yet; `round_matchups.winner_player_id` is
never written. Shipping a weight whose input is always 0.5 is a weight that
silently biases every rating by a constant — harmless here, but a landmine the
day it starts moving.

So S8.3 ships with the term **declared and disabled**, and the active weights
renormalised over the three terms that have data:

```
S8.3 (no combat yet):   power 0.60   count 0.25   hp 0.15   [perf 0.00]
S8.5 (combat lands):    power 0.55   count 0.20   hp 0.10   [perf 0.15]
```

A test asserts the active weights sum to 1 and that `perf` is zero while
`RULES_VERSION` is 1.

### 2.5 A worked example

Round 5 of a four-player match.

| | Board (gamePower) | boardPower | rosterFill | HP | healthShare |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Ali** | 92, 88, 81, 76, 70 | 407/500 = 0.814 | 5/5 = 1.00 | 100 | 1.00 |
| **Can** | 85, 79, 74, 68 | 306/500 = 0.612 | 4/5 = 0.80 | 62 | 0.62 |
| **Mehmet** | 90, 83, 78, 72, 61 | 384/500 = 0.768 | 5/5 = 1.00 | 88 | 0.88 |
| **Emre** | 71, 69, 64, 60, 55 | 319/500 = 0.638 | 5/5 = 1.00 | 45 | 0.45 |

```
Ali    = 0.60(0.814) + 0.25(1.00) + 0.15(1.00) = 0.488 + 0.250 + 0.150 = 0.888
Mehmet = 0.60(0.768) + 0.25(1.00) + 0.15(0.88) = 0.461 + 0.250 + 0.132 = 0.843
Emre   = 0.60(0.638) + 0.25(1.00) + 0.15(0.45) = 0.383 + 0.250 + 0.068 = 0.701
Can    = 0.60(0.612) + 0.25(0.80) + 0.15(0.62) = 0.367 + 0.200 + 0.093 = 0.660
```

Candidate pairings and their power cost (rematch history aside):

```
(Ali,Mehmet)+(Emre,Can)  → |0.888-0.843| + |0.701-0.660| = 0.045 + 0.041 = 0.086   ← cheapest
(Ali,Emre)+(Mehmet,Can)  → 0.187 + 0.183 = 0.370
(Ali,Can)+(Mehmet,Emre)  → 0.228 + 0.142 = 0.370
```

If Ali and Mehmet met last round, the consecutive penalty (500) swamps a
difference of 0.086 and the second-cheapest pairing wins instead. **That is the
intended ordering: not meeting the same person twice running matters far more
than a tight matchup.**

## 3. COMEBACK, RUNAWAY, AND WHERE THE LINE IS

### 3.1 What the simulation says matchmaking can and cannot do

Runaway rate across all three algorithms, 10,000 matches each:

| Players | Pure shuffle | Power-balanced | Hybrid |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3 | 56.5% | 53.6% | 58.9% |
| 4 | 53.6% | 44.9% | 48.9% |
| 5 | 40.3% | 44.0% | 47.3% |

Spread of 12 percentage points between the extremes, with no consistent winner.
**Matchmaking is not the runaway lever**, which agrees with what the S8 economy
work already found: the damage curve moved runaway by 20+ points and a credit
catch-up moved it by 0.0. The chosen algorithm is not chosen for its runaway
number, and the design does not pretend it can be tuned there.

### 3.2 The line between fair matchmaking and skill balancing

The measurable expression of this is **strongest-player exposure**: the average
percentile rank of the opponents the top-rated player faces.

| Algorithm | 4P strongOpp | 5P strongOpp | Reading |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Pure shuffle | 29.3 | 36.2 | The leader mostly draws weaker opponents — luck decides the leader's road |
| Power-balanced | 62.1 | 74.6 | The leader almost always fights the other leader — a hidden handicap |
| **Hybrid** | **46.8** | **57.0** | Slightly above even |

Power-balanced at 74.6 is the failure mode the brief warns about: build a strong
board, and the system quietly ensures you never get an easy round again. It
*reads* as punishment because it is one.

The chosen setting sits just above even. A strong board tends to draw a strong
opponent, but not reliably, and a player can still catch a soft round. Stated as
a rule:

> **Matchmaking may prefer a close matchup. It may never manufacture a hard one.**

Concretely: the power term's weight is **1**, against 500 and 1000 for the
history terms. Rating differences only decide a pairing when the history terms
are tied — which is to say, when the schedule genuinely has a free choice.

### 3.3 What is *not* in the cost function, deliberately

* No term that reads HP and pairs the leader against the healthiest opponent.
* No "the last-placed player gets an easier round."
* No forced mirror of the previous round's result.

Catch-up belongs to events, bounties and the underdog reward — systems the
player can see and reason about — not to a scheduler that works in secret.

## 4. DETERMINISM

The pairing is a pure function:

```
pairRound(matchSeed, roundNo, seats[], history[]) → Pairing[]
```

* Seeded through `createRng(\`matchmaking:${match.seed}:${round}\`)`, the same
  construction S8.2 uses for the round draft. **No `Math.random()`, no
  `Date.now()`, no client input.**
* Every input is server state: `match_players` (HP, elimination), `board_slots`
  and `match_acquisitions` (board), `round_matchups` (history).
* **Ties are broken by seat order, not by the RNG**, so the result does not
  depend on the order the database happened to return rows in. The RNG is used
  only where a genuine coin-flip is wanted, and its draw count is fixed.
* Recomputing the pairing for the same round returns the same answer, so a
  refresh, a reconnect, a duplicate action and a retry all converge.
* The pairing is **written once** and read thereafter — recomputation is a
  fallback for an unwritten round, never an override of a written one. The
  written row is the authority; determinism is what makes rewriting it
  unnecessary rather than what makes it safe.

## 5. SERVER AUTHORITY

The client sends **nothing** for matchmaking. There is no new action.

The `MATCHMAKING` phase is entered by the S8.1 state machine, and the pairing is
computed and written by the server on arrival — exactly the pattern S8.2 uses to
open a round's draft.

| The client cannot | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| choose an opponent | no action carries a player id |
| change a matchup | `round_matchups` has no anon policy and no write endpoint |
| claim a bye | the odd seat is derived from bye history |
| send a rating | ratings are computed from server state only |
| send a seed | the seed is on the match row |
| send a winner | S8.5's business; nothing writes it from a request body |

`ADVANCE_MATCH` remains the only match verb, and it carries no arguments — the
same guarantee S8.1 shipped and S8.2's tests hold.

## 6. DATABASE

### 6.1 Source of truth

| Question | Authority |
| --- | --- |
| Who fought whom, in which round | `round_matchups` |
| What each player owns | `board_slots` (positions) + `match_acquisitions` (record) |
| HP, credits, elimination | `match_players` |
| What happened in the fight | `round_matchups.battle_result` (S8.5) |
| The match's randomness | `matches.seed` |

`round_matchups` is the **only** authority for pairings. The pairing function is
deterministic, but that makes recomputation a *consistency check*, not a second
source — the row is written once and everything downstream reads it.

### 6.2 What `round_matchups` already has, and what it needs

Existing (0030): `id`, `match_id`, `round_no`, `pairing_index`, `player_a`,
`player_b`, `kind`, `battle_result`, `started_at`, `settled_at`,
`winner_player_id`, `damage`, `unique (match_id, round_no, pairing_index)`.

**Deliberately not added, because it would duplicate:**

* `bye_player` — `player_b IS NULL` together with `kind` already says it. A
  third encoding of the same fact is a third thing that can disagree.
* `seed` — derivable as `${match.seed}:${round}`. Storing it invites a row whose
  seed disagrees with its match.
* `rematch_count` — derivable from the rows above it.
* `matchup_id` — `id` is the matchup id.

**Two columns worth adding**, because they are not derivable after the fact:

```sql
alter table round_matchups add column if not exists rating_a numeric(6,4);
alter table round_matchups add column if not exists rating_b numeric(6,4);
```

A rating is a snapshot of a *decision input* at the moment the pairing was made.
The board grows every round, so the rating that produced round 3's pairing
cannot be reconstructed from round 8's board. Recording it is what lets the game
answer "why did I get this opponent" honestly, which §14 requires.

That is the whole of migration **0032** — plus the self-match constraint:

```sql
alter table round_matchups
  add constraint round_matchups_no_self
  check (player_b is null or player_b <> player_a);
```

Additive, idempotent, no drops. Existing rows satisfy the constraint (there are
none with `player_a = player_b`).

## 7. THE ODD SEAT

### 7.1 The measurement that decides this section

Under the chosen algorithm, with a free bye, **the seat that takes the most byes
wins the match far more often than chance**:

| Rule | 3P bye edge | 5P bye edge | comeback | runaway | HP spread |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **FREE** — no damage, no reward | **+20.6 pt** | **+14.5 pt** | 30.3% / 6.5% | 58.9% / 47.3% | 79.9 / 82.0 |
| **CHIP** — flat small damage | +12.5 pt | +9.3 pt | 25.6% / 5.6% | 49.6% / 30.0% | 59.3 / 66.4 |
| **ENCOUNTER** — real fight, 0.6× loss damage | +12.7 pt | +12.5 pt | 23.8% / 5.0% | 54.8% / 42.9% | 72.6 / 77.1 |
| **ENCOUNTER — real fight, full loss damage** | **+6.6 pt** | **+10.9 pt** | 20.6% / 4.3% | 57.2% / 44.0% | 69.8 / 74.8 |

*Bye edge = how much more often the most-byed seat wins than a randomly chosen
seat would. 0 would be neutral.*

A free round is worth **fourteen to twenty-one percentage points of
championship probability**. That is not a rounding error; it is the largest
single effect measured anywhere in S8 outside the damage curve.

### 7.2 The rule

**There is no free round. The odd seat plays an ENCOUNTER.**

* A real `simulateBattle` against a board the **server** assembles from
  `${match.seed}:${round}:encounter`, at the field's median rating.
* Winning pays the participation reward plus a small bonus. Losing costs the
  **full** round damage — the same number a duel would have cost.
* No HP recovery. No board advantage. No extra acquisition.
* `kind = 'ENCOUNTER'`, `player_b IS NULL`, and a `battle_result` in exactly the
  format a duel writes — so the replay, the renderer and the watch screen need
  no special case.

This closes the gap from +20.6 to **+6.6** at three players and from +14.5 to
+10.9 at five. The residue is real and is stated rather than hidden: **not
facing an opponent is inherently a little safer than facing one**, because a
median board is by definition never the strongest board at the table. Pairing
cannot remove it; the second lever is frequency, and §9's algorithm caps the odd
seat at **1–2 rounds out of 8** against 6–7 under the alternatives.

`kind = 'BYE'` stays in the schema as a fallback for a state where an encounter
cannot be built (a pool with too few characters). It is not the normal path, and
a test asserts it is never chosen while an encounter is possible.

### 7.3 Who takes it

Fewest byes so far, then lowest current rating, then seat order. The rating term
is a small nudge, not a rule: it means that when two players have taken the same
number of byes, the one having the worse match gets the gentler round — which is
the only place in matchmaking where catch-up appears at all, and it is bounded
by the bye-count term dominating it.

## 8. ROUND HISTORY

History is `round_matchups` read back — no new table, no counter to keep in step.

```
timesMet(a, b)    = rows where {player_a, player_b} == {a, b}
lastOpponent(p)   = the row at round_no − 1 containing p
byeCount(p)       = rows where player_a = p and player_b is null
```

Every one is a small indexed read against `(match_id, round_no)`, which 0030
already indexes. A derived read cannot drift from the rows it derives from; a
maintained counter can, which is why there is not one.

**Rematch weights** (the cost function of §9):

| Situation | Cost |
| --- | ---: |
| Met in the immediately preceding round | 500 |
| Met once before, not consecutively | 1000 × 1 |
| Met twice before | 1000 × 2 |
| Bye already taken | 300 × count |
| Rating difference | 1 × \|Δ\| |

The magnitudes matter more than the units: history terms are three orders of
magnitude above the power term, so **rating never overrides schedule fairness**.
It decides only among pairings the history is indifferent to.

## 9. ALGORITHMS

### A — Pure deterministic shuffle

Seeded Fisher–Yates over the live seats, pair adjacent, odd seat last.

*For:* trivial, obviously unbiased, obviously deterministic, O(n).
*Against:* no memory. Measured 17.6–29.0% consecutive rematches, and byes land
by luck — **one player took 7 of 8 byes** at three and five players.

### B — Power-balanced

Sort by rating, pair adjacent (1v2, 3v4), odd seat last.

*For:* the tightest matchups (Δrating 0.035 at 5P, the best of the three);
trivially deterministic.
*Against:* it is a hidden handicap system. Strongest-player exposure reaches
**74.6** at five players — build the best board and you never get an easy round
again. Also has no rematch memory (8.4–25.2% consecutive) and the same lumpy
byes (max 6–7).

### C — Hybrid Swiss (chosen)

Exhaustive minimum-cost perfect matching over the §8 cost function, with a
deterministic lexicographic tie-break.

*For:* consecutive rematches **0.0–0.8%**; bye count capped at **1–2** (3–4× more
even than A or B); lowest total rematch rate at five players (37.0%);
strongest-player exposure just above even (46.8 / 57.0).
*Against:* the most code, and it is factorial in table size — but the table is
at most five, which is at most 15 candidate matchings. Exhaustive search is not
merely acceptable here, it is *cheaper* than any heuristic and removes a whole
class of "the approximation picked badly" bugs.

### Comparison

| | A shuffle | B power | **C hybrid** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fairness (Δrating, 5P) | 0.065 | **0.035** | 0.039 |
| Consecutive rematch (4P) | 28.2% | 18.1% | **0.8%** |
| Total rematch (5P) | 46.6% | 49.1% | **37.0%** |
| Bye max, 3P over 8 rounds | 7 | 7 | **3** |
| Bye max, 5P over 8 rounds | 7 | 6 | **2** |
| Strongest-player exposure (5P) | 36.2 | 74.6 | **57.0** |
| Randomness | full | none | tie-breaks only |
| Determinism | yes | yes | yes |
| Complexity | O(n) | O(n log n) | ≤15 enumerations |
| 2/3/4/5 support | yes | yes | yes |

### Why C

Not for its matchup tightness — B is tighter. For the two properties the others
get wrong in ways players would feel within one game:

1. **Nobody sits out seven of eight rounds.** A and B both do this at odd table
   sizes. It is the single worst thing that can happen to somebody who came to
   play.
2. **Nobody plays the same person twice running.** 0.0–0.8% against 8–29%.

And it avoids B's failure the other way: it does not turn a good draft into a
punishment.

## 10. EDGE CASES

| Case | Behaviour |
| --- | --- |
| **2 players** | Short-circuit before scoring: the one pairing, every round. No rematch penalty, no bye. |
| **3 players** | One duel + one encounter. Bye count is the dominant term, so the odd seat rotates 1→2→3. |
| **4 players** | Two duels, no odd seat. |
| **5 players** | Two duels + one encounter. |
| **Eliminated player** | Excluded from `live` before pairing (`eliminated_at is null`). An elimination can change the parity of the table, which is normal, not an error. |
| **Down to one live player** | No pairing. The match ends — the S8.1 state machine already forks to `CHAMPIONSHIP`. |
| **HP = 1** | Still a full participant. HP carries weight 0.10, so a 1 HP player is rated slightly lower and tends to draw a slightly weaker opponent — a nudge, not a rescue. |
| **Identical ratings** | The power term contributes 0 to every candidate, so history terms decide; if those tie too, the lexicographic tie-break does. Deterministic, never a coin flip. |
| **Extreme rating gap** | Allowed. The power term is weight 1 against 500/1000, so it cannot force a mismatch and cannot prevent one that the schedule requires. |
| **Everyone equal** | Reduces to pure schedule fairness: unused pairings first, then bye rotation. |
| **One overwhelming board** | That player draws above-average opponents (exposure ~57 at 5P) but is not quarantined with the other leader every round. |
| **Same matchup as last round** | Cost 500 + 1000. Only chosen when the table has no alternative — which at 2 players is every round, and at 3 players can happen after an elimination. |
| **Round after an encounter** | The encounter is recorded with `player_b IS NULL`, so it raises `byeCount` and lowers that seat's chance of another. It does not count as having met anybody. |
| **Final round / championship** | `MATCHMAKING` is skipped: `ROUND_END → CHAMPIONSHIP` is a separate fork in the S8.1 machine, and the bracket is seeded by HP then round wins. S8.9's work; the `kind` column already carries `PLAY_IN`/`SEMIFINAL`/`FINAL`. |
| **Reconnect / refresh** | Pairings are read from `round_matchups`. A written row is never recomputed. |
| **Duplicate MATCHMAKING** | `unique (match_id, round_no, pairing_index)` plus an existence check under the match row lock: the second writer finds the round already paired and returns `noop`. |
| **Concurrent MATCHMAKING** | Same lock the S8.2 phase advance takes. The loser is told `CONCURRENT_PHASE_ADVANCE`, as S8.2 established. |
| **Stale state** | `p_from` on the phase advance, unchanged from S8.1. |
| **Migration order** | 0032 adds two nullable columns and one check constraint. Code deployed before it must tolerate absent columns — the same `rpcOptional` discipline S8.1 established, and pairing must not *require* `rating_a` to function. |

## 11. ANTI-EXPLOIT

The question is whether a player can choose their opponent by playing badly.

| Lever | Reachable? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Acquisition order** | No | Pairing reads the board's *contents*, not the order it was assembled. |
| **Bid prices** | No | Price is not in the rating. Overpaying and underpaying produce identical ratings for the same character. |
| **Board positioning** | No in S8.3 | Positioning has no combat effect until S8.7. When it lands, positioning must **not** enter the rating — see below. |
| **HP** | Weakly | Losing on purpose lowers the rating by at most 0.15 (weight 0.10 across the full HP range). To move a pairing you must lose real HP, which costs more than the pairing is worth. Self-limiting by construction. |
| **Disconnect / reconnect** | No | Pairing reads persisted state; connection status is not an input. A disconnected player is still paired. |
| **Intentional loss** | Weakly, and it costs more than it gains | The only exploitable term is HP at 0.10. Throwing a round to draw a weaker opponent next round trades ~20 HP for a rating shift of ~0.02 — an order of magnitude below the history terms that actually decide the pairing. |
| **Intentional underpowering** | Weakly | Not buying lowers `rosterFill` and `boardPower`, which does lower the rating. But rounds 1–5 make acquisition mandatory, and from round 6 refusing to buy costs board strength in every future fight. The cure is worse than the disease. |

**The structural defence** is the weight ordering. History terms are 500–1000;
the rating term is 1. A player can only influence the rating term, and the
rating term only decides among pairings that the schedule is already indifferent
to. There is no input a player controls that can override the schedule.

**A rule for later milestones:** positioning must never enter the rating. It is
a per-round, freely re-arrangeable choice, which would make it the one cheap,
zero-cost lever a player could pull to pick an opponent. Board contents cost
credits; position costs nothing.

## 12. TEST PLAN

### Pure (no database)

| Group | Assertions |
| --- | --- |
| **2P** | one pairing every round; no bye; rematch penalty not applied |
| **3P** | one duel + one odd seat; odd seat rotates across 8 rounds; bye count spread ≤1 |
| **4P** | two duels; no odd seat; every seat paired exactly once per round |
| **5P** | two duels + one odd seat; bye count max ≤2 over 8 rounds |
| **No self-match** | over the whole product of table sizes and 8 rounds, `a !== b` always |
| **Coverage** | every live seat appears in exactly one matchup per round |
| **Determinism** | same seed + round + state ⇒ identical output, 1,000 times |
| **Order independence** | shuffling the input seat array does not change the result |
| **No wall clock** | the source contains no `Math.random`, `Date.now`, `new Date` |
| **Consecutive rematch** | over a simulated 8-round match, 0 at 3–5 players |
| **Rematch weighting** | a pairing met twice is never chosen over one met once when both are available |
| **Bye rotation** | the seat with the most byes never takes the next one while another is eligible |
| **Eliminated** | excluded; parity change handled; one survivor ⇒ no pairing |
| **Rating** | weights sum to 1; `perf` weight is 0 while combat is absent; worked example reproduces §2.5 |
| **Rating isolation** | changing a rating cannot change a pairing when the history terms differ |

### Against the database

| Group | Assertions |
| --- | --- |
| **Write once** | pairing a round twice returns `noop` and leaves the rows unchanged |
| **Concurrent** | 2 / 6 / 10 simultaneous entries into `MATCHMAKING` produce exactly one set of rows |
| **Constraint** | a direct insert with `player_a = player_b` is refused by the database |
| **Uniqueness** | a duplicate `(match_id, round_no, pairing_index)` is refused |
| **RLS** | anon reads return nothing |
| **Stale action** | `p_from` mismatch is a `noop` and is reported as `CONCURRENT_PHASE_ADVANCE` |
| **Malicious payload** | phase, round, opponent, rating, seed, winner in a request body change nothing |
| **Legacy** | a legacy game creates no `round_matchups` row |
| **Deploy order** | pairing works with `rating_a` / `rating_b` absent |

### Mutation (≥20)

Remove the self-match guard · remove the consecutive penalty · remove the total
rematch penalty · remove the bye-count term · invert the bye rule (most byes
takes the next) · make the power term dominant (weight 1000) · make the power
term zero · use `Math.random` for the shuffle · use `Date.now` in the seed ·
break the lexicographic tie-break · include eliminated players · exclude live
players · pair a player twice in one round · leave a live player unpaired · allow
`player_b = player_a` in SQL · drop the unique constraint · recompute a written
pairing instead of reading it · let the client send an opponent · let the client
send a rating · enable the `perf` weight before combat exists · put positioning
into the rating · make the encounter a free bye · give the encounter half
damage · skip the bye-count increment for encounters.

## 14. LEGIBILITY: THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE

A player must be able to guess why they got the opponent they got.

The design supports that with **one sentence of explanation per matchup**, drawn
from the term that actually decided it:

> "You haven't faced Can yet."
> "Closest board strength on the table."
> "You played Mehmet last round."
> "Your turn to sit the duel out."

This is not decoration. It is the check on §3's rule: if the honest explanation
for a matchup would be *"because you are winning"*, the system is a handicap and
the design is wrong. `rating_a` / `rating_b` are stored precisely so that the
explanation can be honest after the fact rather than reconstructed.

What is **not** shown: the rating numbers themselves, the cost function, or any
ranking of the table by strength. The player sees a reason, not a scoreboard.

## COMPATIBILITY AUDIT

| System | Interaction | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S8.1 state machine** | `MATCHMAKING` phase exists, is entered from `POSITIONING`, has a 6s deadline, is host- and clock-advanceable | Fits. Pairing is written on arrival, like S8.2's draft. |
| **S8.1 concurrency guard** | `dw_advance_match_phase` holds the match row lock and rejects a losing racer | Reused unchanged. Pairing must be idempotent under it. |
| **S8.2 auction** | Untouched. Pairing reads the board the draft produced. | No interaction. |
| **`match_players`** | HP and `eliminated_at` are pairing inputs | Read-only. |
| **`match_acquisitions`** | The record a rating is computed from | Read-only. |
| **`board_slots`** | Board contents; zones ignored until S8.7 | Read-only. |
| **`round_matchups`** | Written by S8.3; `battle_result` / `winner` / `damage` stay null until S8.5 | Two columns added. |
| **Seed system** | `matchmaking:${match.seed}:${round}` alongside `${seed}:${round}` for the draft | Same construction, different namespace — no collision. |
| **HP / economy** | Matchmaking reads HP; it never writes HP or credits | One-way. |
| **`teamRating` in `battle.ts`** | A different quantity for a different purpose | Not reused, not touched. `RULES_VERSION` stays 1. |
| **Legacy** | A legacy game has no match, so nothing pairs | Untouched. |

**One compatibility risk worth naming:** `recentForm` and positioning are
designed here and have no data until S8.5 / S8.7. They ship disabled with the
weights renormalised, and a test asserts the disabled weight is zero — otherwise
the day combat lands, every rating shifts by a constant nobody remembers
introducing.
